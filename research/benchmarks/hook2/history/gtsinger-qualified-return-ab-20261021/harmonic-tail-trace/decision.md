# Hook-2 prominent trailing-voice investigation, 21 October 2026

The user hears an over-prominent following harmonic/voice through the whole bounce, not only at 4.7–5.4 s. This is a perceptual report; the F0 note-match score does not measure it. **Do not promote the 94.76% pitch-score candidate or suppress harmonics with a global EQ/gate.**

First, the candidate and selected-app-equivalent baseline differ at only 29,022/529,656 stereo sample frames (5.479%). They are sample-identical from 0–4.76 s, 5.41–9.63 s, and 9.67 s to the end. Therefore a whole-song defect cannot have been created *solely* by the new conditional handoff. The exact StudioForge host bounce has one vocal track/insert, and its stereo channels are sample-identical, so this is not evidence of a left/right speaker delay. It does not rule out a renderer artifact present in both versions.

Second, a research-only trace export added `dry_high_residual_mix`, `high_frequency_evidence`, and `periodic_coherence` columns to `PluginProcessor.cpp`. Rendering with the new trace fields produced the **exact selected-app-equivalent WAV SHA-256** `1990aca4f64d96cc40abe4aedd3aadc932e6a551b234ccc851342ec23d88590d`; the measurement did not alter the audio. The selected listening app's embedded VST3 SHA-256 remains `617682fa530632e3418793eb3cfb733b2a1359f36082672ce9237440935fbd20`.

Across 16,551 logged 32-sample frames, 13,108 have wet mix >0.9. Of those, the dry-high residual is exactly zero through the 99th percentile; only 53 frames have residual mix >0.05, concentrated near consonant/transition regions around 4 s and 7 s. The 1–4 s and 5–6 s bins are essentially full-wet throughout. `Voiced Colour` is default 0% in this runner. This rules out a persistent *explicit dry-high blend* or the optional colour stage as the explanation for a full-song following layer. Dry/wet crossfades occur around some boundaries, but not in the continuous full-wet passages.

Exploratory steady-window FFTs also do **not** show a consistent global increase in normal harmonics H2–H8 relative to H1: Artist/Vibe values are -6.21/-6.49 dB at 1.2–1.6 s, +5.26/+5.21 dB at 2.7–3.1 s, +6.96/+6.38 dB at 3.3–3.7 s, and +0.93/+0.82 dB at 9.2–9.6 s. These fixed-window values are not a perceptual score. The observation argues against simply reducing all overtones; the audible complaint is more consistent with *time-varying secondary texture* than uniform harmonic excess. The exact source of that texture (grain phase/marking, overlap-add, or an unmeasured processing path) is **not yet proven**.

Next discriminating experiment: on a few sustained vowels with the dry-high residual = 0 and wet mix = 1, compare short-time harmonic-sideband energy and pulse-to-pulse phase consistency to Artist, with both files loudness-matched and on the same raw clock. Then vary one renderer factor in a separate uninstalled research build, demanding lower secondary texture without worse F0 transitions, formant, consonants, clicks or latency. No installed app or production DSP should change on the present evidence.
